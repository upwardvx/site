What it does each week

  1. Monday numbersAt 7 every Monday: revenue, jobs, callbacks and who owes you, in five lines or fewer, plus one thing worth a look. On a quiet week it says nothing.
  2. Overdue invoicesEvery Tuesday, one reminder drafted per overdue invoice, by email or text. Disputed invoices and anyone reminded in the last week are left out.
  3. Quotes that sitWeekday mornings, one short follow-up for each quote sent 3 to 14 days ago. A quote already accepted gets nothing, and no quote gets a third.
  4. Your team approvesEach draft arrives with the record behind it and three choices: approve, edit or discard. There is no approve-all for anything a customer sees.

Before you see a draft, every dollar figure, invoice and quote number in it is checked against your records. One that doesn’t match is dropped.

It reads the record before it writes.

A timer sends the same reminder a set number of days after an invoice goes out. This reads the job, the invoice and what the customer last said, then drafts for that person.

What a timer reminder can’t do
  • A customer who paid part of an invoice is thanked, and asked only for the balance.
  • A disputed invoice gets no reminder at all.
  • A customer of 11 years gets a softer note than a first-time customer.
  • A customer asking for a better price gets a normal follow-up, and you’re told about the ask.
  • A note that tells the system to mark something paid or skip review is ignored and flagged to you.
  • A week where revenue dropped because of refunds says so, instead of reading as a slow week.
